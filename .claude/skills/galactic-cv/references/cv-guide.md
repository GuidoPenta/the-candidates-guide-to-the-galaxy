# Galactic CV Guide — operational checklist

Purpose: apply Guido Penta's CV rules to a developer's CV, section by section, as checklists.

Source: https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers (MIT, © 2023 Guido Penta) — full guide there, this is an operational summary.
- English version: https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md
- Italian version (original, slightly richer): https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/index.md
- Companion typography guide recommended by the guide itself: Simon Di Fresco, *Your CV looks like sh\*t* — https://github.com/SimonDiff/devs-cv-typography-guidelines (CC0). Its rules are in `recruiter-rubric.md`, Block C.

> **Scope warning (from the guide).** The guide is written for the **Italian** job market. Rules
> tied to Italy (the data-processing authorization, the photo being a free choice) must be
> flagged as such when the user targets another country. Do not silently apply them elsewhere;
> do not replace them with invented local rules either — say the guide does not cover that market.

---

## 0. Guiding stance

Apply these as framing before any section-level check:

- **The perfect CV does not exist, and neither does the "ATS-proof CV".** Never promise either.
- **The CV is a showcase of yourself, not a sterile shopping list** of technologies,
  languages and certifications. It must *tell* who you are — concise but coherent.
- **Content is king, but the eye wants its share too.** Layout and aesthetics matter.
- **Recruiters read all CVs.** The ATS is a tool for repetitive, low-value tasks (CV database,
  multiposting, filtering the CVs "closest" to the ad); the idea of a purely "automatic reading"
  is a myth. Keywords matter because they help your CV surface, not because a robot decides.
- **Invest a few hours** in building the CV: it is the first business card that reaches the
  people who will likely hire you and work with you.

Obstacles to watch for while writing (Italian version, drawn from a post by Roberta Zantedeschi —
use them as a self-check with the user):
- little self-knowledge; low esteem of your own value;
- unclear ideas about what you seek, who you are talking to, which direction you want;
- urgency (writing it only when in need);
- little care for structure, information hierarchy and visual design;
- trouble selecting the information that adds value;
- the "fundamentalist" belief that the CV must fit one page **at all costs**;
- the belief that LinkedIn makes the CV useless (wrong), or that a PDF alone is enough to find a job;
- not putting yourself in the reader's shoes;
- only describing, never telling a story;
- excluding anything personal — or overdoing personal facts;
- job-description / corporate-memo language (writing quality matters, not just grammar).

## 1. General rules  ([§1](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#1-general-info))

- [ ] **Aim for one page.** An experienced recruiter reads a CV in 10–20 seconds. (Aim, not dogma:
      see the "at all costs" obstacle above.)
- [ ] **Concise but comprehensive**: contains all the keywords needed for the target position.
- [ ] **Language matches the job ad.** Ad in English → CV in English; ad in Italian → CV in Italian.
- [ ] **Keep two language versions** of the CV ready.
- [ ] **No Europass.** Guido "STRONGLY" advises against it: outdated, no graphic customization.
- [ ] Build it with a real template tool: Canva (the example CV), LaTeX templates, or Overleaf.
- [ ] Minimal aesthetic is fine — the example uses a very minimal template "that works".

## 2. Photo  ([§2](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#2-the-photo))

Guido's answer to "photo or not?": **"do as you please"** — no defined rule; removing it may
reduce some aesthetic bias but not all bias. If the user includes one:

- [ ] Half-length, professionally taken, facing the camera or slightly to the side.
- [ ] NOT grainy, NOT very old.
- [ ] NOT at the beach, at a party, holding cocktails / dogs / kittens / anything.
- [ ] NOT a (badly) cropped group photo.
- [ ] NOT winking, skimpy or provocative — "It's a CV, not a modeling book."
- [ ] No professional photo? An avatar / illustration of yourself is fine ("you're still a dev"),
      as long as it stays professional.

Never judge the user's appearance. Only check the rules above.

## 3. Contacts  ([§3](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#3-contacts))

Missing email/phone is, in Guido's words, "INSANE" — this section is FUNDAMENTAL.

- [ ] Email address present **and** mobile number present.
- [ ] **Professional email** (no `devils88` / `bananakiller83`).
- [ ] LinkedIn, GitHub, and online portfolio (if any) included.
- [ ] Links are **clickable** (no copy-paste needed).
- [ ] **Placed at the top**, immediately under the photo / header — never at the bottom of the CV
      (Italian version: "contacts must be immediately visible and usable").
- [ ] All useful contacts, as in the example (mobile, location, email, GitHub, LinkedIn).

## 4. About Me  ([§4](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#4-about-me))

"To say the least, FUNDAMENTAL" — one of the first things evaluators read. A few lines that
summarize who you are, what you did, and above all what you are looking for.

Checklist (modelled on the Luke Skywalker example):
- [ ] Current job title.
- [ ] Technologies/languages you know **or are learning**.
- [ ] Scope of responsibility if any (e.g. "I manage a small team of 3").
- [ ] **What you are looking for next** (work and personal goals).
- [ ] Related technical interests (e.g. UX/UI and accessibility for a front-end dev) and how
      background connects to them.
- [ ] Optional "extras": personal side projects, hobbies — they are conversation hooks.

Example structure (from the guide's sample CV, paraphrased):
> Lead Frontend Developer, experienced in JS, HTML, CSS, React, Next.js, Angular. Manages a small
> team and is looking for a company where they can grow technical and managerial skills. Strongly
> interested in UX/UI and accessibility, given a psychology degree. + two lines of hobbies.

## 5. Professional experience  ([§5](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#5-professional-experiences))

"The heart of the whole CV." Goal: the right compromise between **concise** and **exhaustive**,
highlighting the most relevant projects and technologies.

- [ ] **Standardized layout** across all entries (same structure every time → more readable).
- [ ] Every entry has **start and end dates** and the **company name**.
- [ ] **Consistent professional growth is visible**: the sequence of roles shows progression
      (example: Frontend Developer 2012–2015 → Senior Frontend Developer 2015–2020 → Lead
      Frontend Developer 2020–current). An experienced recruiter spots this immediately.
- [ ] **Bullet points** that succinctly but effectively describe: key duties, projects followed,
      technologies used, anything relevant **to the specific position** sought.
- [ ] Junior / less structured profiles: still show growth and responsibilities (the guide says
      the same things can be highlighted effectively with fewer years).

Example entry shape (from the sample CV):
```
Lead Frontend Developer
Death Star srl - 2020/Current (Full remote)
Lead Frontend Developer in an international company working on Finance/Home Banking projects.
My main tasks are:
• FE platform improvements
• Keeping an eye on the Padawan team
• Data Management (API, templates)
• Issues/bug resolutions
```
Pattern: **role title → company · dates (· work mode) → one-line context (company type, domain) →
bullets of duties/projects/tech.**

## 6. Education / training  ([§6](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#6-training-experiences))

- [ ] **Senior profile**: degree only, with **grade** and **thesis title** (the thesis is a
      potential conversation starter).
- [ ] **Junior profile**: also include the high-school diploma track and grade (if not a
      graduate: diploma only).
- [ ] **Ongoing studies** (university, master's, PhD) MUST be listed — e.g. to access curricular
      internships, and because you will need study leave for exams: the evaluator must know.
- [ ] **Academy / IT bootcamp** counts as training and must be listed, with: technologies being
      studied, **expected end date**, and the hands-on project(s).

## 7. Tech skills  ([§7](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#7-tech-skills))

"Along with professional experience, the heart of any CV."

- [ ] Neat, concise list of acquired skills — and skills in progress may be flagged
      ("work in progress").
- [ ] **No stars / progress bars** as proficiency indicators: many ATSs may not recognize
      graphic signs. (The guide mentions progress bars as a graphic option, then warns about ATS;
      when in doubt, use text.)
- [ ] **Ordered by strength / daily use first**, coherent with the target role: applying as FE →
      FE languages/frameworks first; BE languages, databases and others last.
- [ ] Contains **all the keywords** the company/selector is looking for, so the ATS can surface
      the CV among the others.

## 8. Soft skills  ([§8](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#8-soft-skills))

Guido's position: a professional is built **50% hard skills, 50% soft skills**. Dev work is not
robotic; it has a large human component (communication, teamworking, empathy, proactivity).

- [ ] Soft skills section **present**.
- [ ] **STOP with the usual clichés.** List "real" soft skills that fit the person's actual
      personality.
- [ ] **Honesty check**: a meticulous person who needs order and routine probably does not work
      well under stress/speed — writing "I work well under pressure" is disingenuous (and a cliché).
- [ ] Do the "mental exercise": ask the user which soft skills really characterize them.
      They are great conversation starters.

## 9. Courses and certifications  ([§9](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#9-courses-and-certifications))

- [ ] Include completed courses and recognized certifications — some positions require specific
      ones as mandatory.
- [ ] They also show personal commitment to growing technical skills.
- [ ] "Aspirational" certifications (Scrum / Agile / PM) are valuable: Guido uses them as
      conversation starters signalling where the person wants to grow ("that's what I want to do
      when I grow up") — and he appreciates it.

## 10. Publications  ([§10](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#10-publications))

- [ ] If the user contributed to journals, conferences, or published online articles, list them
      (essential for academic careers, valued elsewhere: they show competence and ability to
      analyze complex, innovative problems).
- [ ] Each entry: **title; publication date; list of authors; institution/magazine; DOI**.

## 11. Languages and hobbies  ([§11](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#11-languages-and-hobbies))

Hobbies — Guido's most distinctive opinion: 90% of CVs he receives lack them, "a huge shame". For
him it is the most interesting (sometimes fun) section: it shows the professional as a person and
gives many conversation starters.
- [ ] **Hobbies section present.** Encourage the user to add one.
- [ ] Hobbies tell something about you (examples from the guide: extreme sports → pushing limits,
      curiosity, courage; team sports → teamwork, not a "lone wolf"). Do not fabricate the
      interpretation for the user; just note they will be read this way.

Languages:
- [ ] At least one language besides the native one (preferably English) — Guido considers it
      FUNDAMENTAL.
- [ ] Level stated for each language (the example uses "mother tongue", "B1").
- [ ] Language certifications included, if any.

## 12. Data-processing authorization (Italy)  ([§12](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#12-data-processing-authorization))

- Legislative Decree 101/2018: consent to processing personal data in the CV is **no longer
  mandatory** in Italy. Guido still **recommends including it**, because:
  - it authorizes employer, HR and recruiters to process the data protected by Italian privacy law
    and the GDPR;
  - it lets the employer contact you right away and keep your CV for future selections;
  - without it the employer must ask for authorization first → delays and bureaucracy.
- [ ] Italian applications: authorization sentence present at the bottom.
- [ ] Non-Italian applications: flag that this rule is Italy-specific in the guide.

Sample sentence used in the example CV (Italian law references as written there):
> I authorize the processing of my personal data in the CV in accordance with Legislative Decree
> No. 196 of June 30, 2003 "Code on the Protection of Personal Data" and Article 13 of the GDPR
> (EU Regulation 2016/679).

## 13. Final considerations  ([§13](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-CV-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#13-final-considerations))

- [ ] Does the CV tell a coherent story (experience first, then education, passions, soft skills,
      and the wish to find the "right place")?
- [ ] Is it more than a list of technologies/languages/certifications?

## Section order used in the example CV

Left column: photo → **Contacts** → Tech Skills → Certifications → Soft Skills → Languages.
Right column: **Name + current title** → About Me → Work Experiences (most recent first) →
Training Experiences → Hobbies and Interests → data-processing authorization (small, italic).

The guide does not mandate a two-column layout; it is the chosen template. For a single-column
Markdown output use: Header (name, title, contacts) → About Me → Experience → Tech Skills →
Soft Skills → Education → Certifications → Publications → Languages → Hobbies → Authorization.
(This linear order is an adaptation, not a rule from the guide.)

## Common mistakes (as called out by the guide)

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| No email and/or phone | Add both, at the top |
| Contacts at the bottom of the CV | Move them to the top, immediately visible |
| Unprofessional email | Use a name-based address |
| Non-clickable LinkedIn/GitHub | Make links clickable |
| Europass format | Use a real template (Canva, LaTeX, Overleaf) |
| CV language ≠ ad language | Match the ad; keep two versions |
| Stars / progress bars for skills | Plain text list, strongest first |
| Skills in random order | Strongest / daily-use first, role-relevant first |
| Cliché soft skills ("works well under pressure") | Real, personality-specific ones |
| No hobbies section | Add one — conversation starters |
| Ongoing studies / bootcamp omitted | List them with expected end date |
| Unprofessional photo (party, beach, cropped group, provocative) | Professional half-length, or avatar, or none |
| CV as a shopping list of tech | Tell a concise, coherent story |
| Omitting the thesis title (graduates) | Add it — talking point |

## Notes

- The guide's sample bullets describe **duties** ("FE platform improvements"), not measured
  results. Guido does not require metrics; the recruiter rubric (Block A3 in
  `recruiter-rubric.md`) does reward measured results. When both apply, keep Guido's structure
  and add a result only if the user supplies one — never invent numbers.
- The sample CV is intentionally goliardic (Star Wars); copy its structure, not its jokes.
