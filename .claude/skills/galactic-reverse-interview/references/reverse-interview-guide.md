## 1. Why you must ask questions (from the guide)

Guido's position: as a candidate you not only *can* ask questions, you **must**. Asking is useful to:

- gather as much information as possible about the company whose hiring process you are entering;
- show genuine interest in the company;
- get the most complete picture possible of the role, the duties, the team and the other things that matter;
- avoid going silent at the classic "so, do you have any questions for us?".

The underlying stance: at the time of writing (2023), in Guido's view, candidates had negotiating
power (in tech and many other sectors), because companies struggled to find motivated, qualified people. So **the company
is not the only one choosing** — the candidate can (and, in Guido's opinion, must) choose the right
company for their personal and professional moment.

The same stance appears in the negotiation guide: "interviews are always a two-way street" and "ASK
QUESTIONS. You're not just there to answer; you're there to ask!"
([negotiation guide, section 3](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-negotiation-guide-for-developers/blob/main/docs/english.md#3-play-your-cards-right)).
Good questions reveal growth potential and how much the company values its developers.

## 2. How many and which ones (from the guide)

- **Don't ask them all** — otherwise the interview "would last about 7 hours".
- **Pick the subset that best fits the company** you are approaching.
- **Do your homework first:** the guide asks you to pick questions "considerando anche che ormai
  tantissime informazioni sull'azienda si possono facilmente reperire online". ([pack]: some bank
  questions may already be answered publicly; ask them only to verify.) In Italy, company financial statements (*bilanci*) are often public and free —
  the negotiation guide explicitly suggests checking them: "you don't want to be hired by a
  company that is going bankrupt, right?"
  ([negotiation guide IT, section 2](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-negotiation-guide-for-developers/blob/main/README.md#2-prima-del-colloquio-prepara-il-terreno)).

## 3. The question bank

Tags (skill-pack adaptation, **not in the original** — the guide does not assign questions to
interviewer types; these are suggestions on who is most likely to answer well):

| Tag | Interviewer |
|---|---|
| `[HR]` | HR / recruiter / talent acquisition |
| `[ENG]` | engineer / peer / tech lead in a technical round |
| `[MGR]` | hiring manager / engineering manager |
| `[FND]` | founder / CEO / C-level (small companies, final rounds) |
| `[WEB]` | often answerable by research before the interview — ask only if not public or to verify |

### 3.1 About the company (*Informazioni sull'azienda*)

1. What are your company's vision and mission? `[FND] [MGR] [WEB]`
2. Is the industry the company operates in economically healthy? `[FND] [MGR]`
3. What was the company's revenue over the last two years? `[FND] [HR] [WEB]`
4. What revenue do you expect this year / next year? `[FND]`
5. Do you have an internal ladder that regulates professional / salary growth? `[HR] [MGR]`
6. Does the company sponsor / support Open Source projects? `[ENG] [MGR]`
7. Does the company sponsor / support / encourage its people to speak at industry events? `[ENG] [MGR]`
8. In your view, what are the biggest challenges of the industry you operate in? `[FND] [MGR]`
9. What % of staff turnover did you have last year? `[HR] [MGR]`
10. What is the planned investment budget for the area I would join? `[MGR] [FND]`
11. Is the company a partner / sponsor of projects with a social impact? `[HR] [FND] [WEB]`
12. What is company life like, and how does it embody the values that define you? `[HR] [ENG]`

### 3.2 How work is organized (*Organizzazione del lavoro*)

13. What does a typical day look like in your company? `[ENG] [MGR]`
14. Are working hours the typical 9-to-6, or do you allow flexibility? `[HR] [MGR]`
15. Is work organized by objectives (i.e., without any fixed schedule)? `[MGR] [HR]`
16. How is the onboarding path structured? `[HR] [MGR]`
17. Who, within the company or my future team, decides priorities? `[MGR] [ENG]`
18. What daily tasks will I be doing? `[MGR] [ENG]`
19. How many calls / meetings are typically scheduled in a week? `[ENG]`
20. Is there a chance to do pair programming? `[ENG]`
21. How does the personal / professional review process work? `[MGR] [HR]`
22. How much does the company value work-life balance? `[HR] [ENG]`
23. What development methodology do you use? (Agile, Scrum, etc.) `[ENG] [MGR]`

### 3.3 The tools (*I tools*)

24. Can I choose the computer I work best with (PC / Mac)? `[HR] [MGR]`
25. What task-management tool do you use? `[ENG]`
26. Which version of (technology / language / framework) do you use? `[ENG]`
27. How did you arrive at the technology stack currently in use? `[ENG] [MGR]`
28. Do you usually write technical documentation? `[ENG]`
29. Do you use version control? If so, which one? `[ENG]`
30. Do you do software quality control? `[ENG]`
31. Do you write unit tests? `[ENG]`
32. Do you have automated tests? `[ENG]`

### 3.4 Contract, bureaucracy and pay (*CCNL, burocrazia e parte economica*)

33. Which CCNL (national collective labour agreement) do you apply? `[HR]`
34. What benefits are there? (meal vouchers, performance bonuses, corporate welfare, psychotherapy
    sessions, wellness partnerships, other) `[HR]`
35. Is there a budget for the home office? `[HR] [MGR]`
36. Is there a personal training budget? `[HR] [MGR]`
37. Is there a budget for certifications? `[HR] [MGR]`

> **[pack] note**, Italy context: the CCNL determines level (*livello*), minimums, holidays, leave
> and more. **[Guido]** The negotiation guide notes the contract level affects "holidays, TFR
> (*trattamento di fine rapporto*), maternity/paternity and future protections"
> ([section 4](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-negotiation-guide-for-developers/blob/main/README.md#4-negoziare-sul-serio)).
> **[pack]** Outside Italy, ask the equivalent: which contract/collective agreement or employment terms apply.
> Hand the answers to this block to the **galactic-negotiation** skill — they feed the total-compensation comparison.

### 3.5 My place in your company (*Il mio posto nella vostra azienda*)

38. Are there specific goals you expect me to reach in the first 30 / 60 / 90 days? `[MGR]`
39. Is there a model for evaluating my performance / the goals I reach? `[MGR] [HR]`
40. Will I have the chance to do 1:1s with my manager / senior / tech lead / supervisor? `[MGR] [ENG]`
41. How will the team I'm joining be composed? `[MGR] [ENG]`
42. Are there particular critical issues the team is facing right now? `[MGR] [ENG]`
43. Why should I choose your company? `[FND] [MGR] [HR]`
44. Why should you choose me? What struck you positively? `[MGR] [HR]`

## 4. Using the bank (skill-pack guidance)

Everything in this section is an **adaptation by the skill pack**, built on the guide's two rules
("don't ask them all", "research first"). It is not text from Guido's guide.

### 4.1 Building a short list per round

1. Remove every `[WEB]` question you can answer yourself from the company site, careers page,
   LinkedIn, Glassdoor reviews, public financial statements.
2. Match the remaining questions to the person in front of you using the tags. A peer engineer is
   the best source for #19 (meetings), #20, #25–#32; HR for #33–#37; the hiring manager for
   #17, #18, #38–#42; a founder for #2–#4, #8, #10.
3. Keep **3–5 questions per round**, prioritized, plus 1–2 spares in case they are answered
   during the conversation. Never repeat a question already answered — refer back to it instead
   ("you mentioned X earlier — how does that work in practice?").
4. Spread the bank across rounds: the same question asked to two different people (e.g. #22
   work-life balance to HR and to a peer) is a useful consistency check.
5. Always have at least one question ready for the "any questions for us?" moment — one of the
   guide's stated reasons for asking is not going silent ("non fare scena muta").

### 4.2 Reading the answers

Signals worth noting (again: skill-pack guidance, derived from what each question probes):

- **Vague or deflected answers** to concrete questions (turnover %, who sets priorities,
  30/60/90 goals, review process) — ask once more, then record it as "unknown", not as "fine".
- **Inconsistency between interviewers** (HR says flexible hours, the engineer describes daily
  late evenings) — trust the person who does the job.
- **Hours / flexibility**: the negotiation guide warns about the "young and dynamic company
  looking for people who work 'without schedules' (aka: you stay until 8 pm every day)" and
  "real or fake smart working" ([IT section 4](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-negotiation-guide-for-developers/blob/main/README.md#4-negoziare-sul-serio)).
- **Contract type**: if the answer to #33 reveals you'd be a VAT-number contractor (P.IVA) with
  fixed hours and a single client, the negotiation guide calls it "disguised subordinate work"
  and often illegal ([IT section 9](https://github.com/GuidoPenta/galactic-negotiation-guide-for-developers/blob/main/README.md#9-bonus-track-3-freelance)).
- **Engineering hygiene** (#28–#32): "no" to docs, version control or tests isn't automatically a
  deal-breaker, but ask whether there is a plan — that's what tells you about the role's workload.
- **No onboarding / no 1:1s / no review model** (#16, #21, #39, #40): growth will depend on you
  asking for it; relevant input for the negotiation (onboarding is listed there as negotiable).
- **#44 "Why should you choose me?"** — the answer tells you what they value in you; reuse it
  later as leverage when negotiating.

### 4.3 After the interview

- Write down answers the same day, per interviewer, with the question number.
- Anything economic or contractual (#5, #33–#37, hours, remote) goes to the negotiation step.
- Unanswered critical questions become follow-ups by email to HR.

## 5. Translation fixes vs the English doc

- IT "*Quanto ha fatturato l'azienda negli ultimi due anni?*" is about **revenue**; the English
  doc renders it as "How much turnover has the company had in the past two years?", which in
  English reads as staff turnover. This file uses "revenue" (#3, #4) and keeps staff turnover as a
  separate question (#9), as in the Italian original.
- IT "*Fate contratto qualità del software?*" (likely a typo for *controllo qualità*); the English
  doc says "software quality contracting". Rendered here as "software quality control" (#30).
- The English doc omits: typical day (#13), PC/Mac choice (#24), development methodology (#23).
  They are restored from the Italian original.
- "CCNL" (#33) is Italian-specific; the English doc says "collective bargaining agreement".
